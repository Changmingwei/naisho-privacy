Talk to the person beside you. Headphones on.

Two people, both wearing AirPods with noise cancelling on, cannot hear each other. Open Naisho and the two phones connect directly — each voice goes straight into the other person's ears. Nothing to take out, nothing to turn off.

No server, no account. Your voice goes from one phone to the other and nowhere else.

Why

This happens more often than you'd think

  • On the train — Sitting side by side, both with noise cancelling on. One sentence costs you an earbud — and you wait for them to take one out too.
  • On a run — One of you ahead of the other, wind louder than either of you. Shout, and they still only hear their music.
  • In a library — Talking means dropping your voice — right down to where the other person can't hear it either.
  • In an open-plan office — One desk apart, and saying it out loud says it to the whole floor.

Every one of them starts with the same move: taking a headphone out. Naisho does one thing — it removes that move.

What it does

Six things, all of them true

01

The two phones connect to each other

Your voice does not pass through a server — we don't have one. Whichever link is working: no cellular, no router, no data used at all. It works in a tunnel.

The links are Wi‑Fi Aware, peer-to-peer Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth.

02

No account, and nothing is kept

Nothing to sign up for, no email, no phone number. What you say is never recorded, never transcribed, never stored — when you stop, it is gone.

The only thing that ever leaves your phone is a diagnostic report, and only if you send one or leave automatic crash reports on.

03

You can both talk at once

Full duplex — there is no button to hold down. Music drops to the background while someone is speaking and comes back up after; podcasts pause and resume.

The microphone is live only during a conversation, and is released the moment it ends.

04

You can see who is nearby

Open it and you see which friends are around right now. Ultra Wideband and Bluetooth make "nearby" a great deal more accurate than guessing from signal strength.

If their Naisho is closed, you can wake it.

05

Your friend list stays on your phone

Who your friends are and when you last talked lives on that phone only and is never uploaded. On a new phone, you become friends again.

Your nickname and avatar emoji are broadcast to people nearby with Naisho open — that is what "seeing who is nearby" is.

06

Connecting takes one tap from each of you

The first time, one of you taps the other and the other taps Confirm and join. Nothing connects until both have — nobody gets to connect to you on their own.

That is where a friendship starts, and you can remove every one of them at any time in Settings.

To be precise

Encryption, as two separate things

Voice

Every voice link between the two phones is encrypted. Apple's Multipeer link is encrypted by requirement, and each of the three links we build ourselves carries its own session key.

Wake

This one is not encrypted. Waking a friend is a signed Bluetooth broadcast: the signature proves it really came from a friend of yours, but the contents are in the clear, and they carry the sender's nickname and avatar emoji. Anything listening nearby can read them.

We write them as two things because they are two things. The full account is in the privacy policy.

Naisho does nothing on one phone.

It has to be on both, and it only works at close range. That is not a limitation waiting to be fixed — it is what is left when you take out the server, the account and the recording.

Naisho is 内緒 in Japanese: a thing said quietly, to one person. What it gives you is a small space with two people in it.
